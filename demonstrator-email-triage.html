Assisted Email
Triage Demonstrator

Many organisations rely on shared mailboxes to manage enquiries, referrals, requests and general correspondence. Over time this becomes difficult to manage — messages get missed, responses vary between staff, and there is often no clear record of how an enquiry was handled. Adjona Technology built a Microsoft 365 proof of concept to show how a structured assisted triage process can improve visibility, consistency and governance.

The problem

Shared mailboxes are hard to manage.

Shared mailboxes are often used as informal front doors into an organisation. They may receive volunteer enquiries, referral requests, service user messages, complaints, supplier emails, general information requests or internal support queries. Without a structured process, the same set of problems tends to emerge.

01
Messages missed or left unactioned because there is no clear ownership or visibility of what has arrived.
02
Responses varying between staff or volunteers, with no consistent approach to common enquiry types.
03
No clear record of how enquiries were handled, making handover, oversight and reporting difficult.
04
Difficulty prioritising — all enquiries sit in the same inbox regardless of urgency or type.
05
Limited management visibility of volumes, response times or whether similar enquiries are being handled consistently.
The demonstrator

A five-step assisted triage workflow.

Adjona built a Microsoft 365 proof of concept showing how a shared mailbox triage process can be made more structured, visible and consistent — using tools many organisations are already paying for. The workflow follows five steps from arrival to staff review.

01
Email arrives
An enquiry-style email arrives in a test triage mailbox. Power Automate monitors the mailbox and triggers the workflow automatically.
02
Triage logic applied
The workflow identifies the broad type of enquiry based on subject, sender or content — categorising it for routing and review.
03
Outcome recorded in SharePoint
The triage result is written automatically into a SharePoint list, creating a structured log of every enquiry received.
04
Suggested responses associated
Relevant suggested responses, useful links or internal next actions can be associated with different enquiry types — giving staff a consistent starting point.
05
Staff review and act
Staff review the triage log before deciding what action to take. Human judgement remains at the centre of every decision.
Shared Mailbox
The single point of entry for incoming enquiries, monitored continuously by Power Automate.
Power Automate
Applies triage logic, routes enquiries and writes structured records without manual intervention.
SharePoint
Stores the triage log and holds the response library associated with each enquiry type.
Microsoft Teams
Optional notifications or approval steps where an enquiry needs immediate attention or escalation.
Governance

Assisted triage, not automatic decision-making.

This proof of concept does not remove human review. The workflow organises incoming information, suggests likely categories and points staff towards relevant responses or next actions. Staff remain responsible for reviewing each enquiry and deciding what should happen.

This matters wherever an enquiry may involve personal information, complaints, safeguarding concerns, vulnerable people, legal issues or anything unusual. The workflow is designed to support judgement — not replace it.

Automation should support judgement, not replace it. The demonstrator is built around this principle. Every enquiry in the triage log is reviewed by a person before any action is taken.

For organisations handling enquiries from service users, clients, patients or members of the public, the governance implications of how incoming messages are managed are significant. An assisted triage workflow can help create a more consistent, auditable and accountable process — without introducing unnecessary automation risk.

A production version would need to consider
Role-based access to the triage log
SharePoint permissions and sharing controls
Retention of enquiry records
Handling of personal data in email content
Escalation routes for sensitive enquiries
DPIA requirements where appropriate
Staff training and adoption
Review and audit of triage logic
What it improves

Better visibility, consistency and oversight.

An assisted triage workflow addresses the practical operational problems that build up around unstructured shared mailbox use. The improvements are immediate and measurable.

Visibility of incoming enquiries — staff can see what has arrived, what is waiting and what has been actioned
Consistency of suggested responses — common enquiry types point to the same starting point every time
Handover between staff or volunteers — the triage log gives anyone picking up the mailbox an immediate picture
Basic audit trail — a structured record of every enquiry received and how it was categorised
Prioritisation — enquiry types can be flagged or routed differently based on category or urgency
Reporting and service improvement — volume and category data becomes available for management and governance purposes
Who this could help
