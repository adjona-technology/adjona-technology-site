Executive summary

This example illustrates how a review might translate configuration observations into management decisions. The fictional organisation has 28 staff, 12 volunteer collaborators and a small outsourced IT team. All figures and observations below are invented for demonstration; no tenant was inspected. The example identifies three high, three medium and two lower priority findings. These ratings are illustrative, not a measured security score.

The immediate priorities are to establish ownership of privileged access, validate access to restricted information, and confirm whether former collaborators still need access. The next stage is to agree responsibilities, scope remediation and verify completion. No changes have been performed.

Scope and limitations

Illustrative scope: Microsoft Entra identities and administrative roles; Teams and SharePoint ownership and lifecycle; guest access and sharing; governance records; AI readiness.

Exclusions: endpoint compliance assessment, penetration testing, full licensing audit, legal compliance opinion, Cyber Essentials certification and review of all individual files.

A real engagement would document evidence sources, permissions, sampling, date and coverage. Lack of evidence must not be treated as proof that a control is absent.

Prioritisation method

High
Plausible material exposure or control failure requiring prompt validation and management attention.
Medium
Meaningful weakness requiring planned action.
Lower
Improvement in clarity, maintainability or evidence.

Ratings consider sensitivity, likelihood, impact and compensating controls; they are subject to validation. Suggested time bands below are illustrative, not contractual commitments.

Management decisions

  1. Who is accountable for Microsoft 365 governance and escalation?
  2. Which information requires restricted access and who approves it?
  3. Who is authorised to commission and approve remediation?
  4. What evidence should be reviewed by trustees and at what frequency?
