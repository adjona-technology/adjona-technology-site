Financial resilience must begin before the emergency

September 2026
12 min read
Adjona Technology

The Charity Commission has warned that some financially distressed charities are approaching the regulator only when their continued operation is already under threat.

Requests for regulatory authority more than doubled during April to June compared with the same period in the previous year. Some charities sought urgent permission for transactions such as property disposals when their financial options had already become severely limited.

This is not simply a finance-department problem. It is a governance warning.

Trustees need reliable information, agreed warning thresholds and enough time to consider their options before a difficult financial position becomes an emergency.

Microsoft 365 cannot solve financial distress. It can, however, help a charity make warning indicators visible, assign responsibility, escalate missed actions and preserve evidence of the decisions trustees have made.

Financial information must lead to decisions

Trustees are responsible for safeguarding their charity's assets and ensuring that its resources are used to pursue its charitable purposes.

Receiving a set of accounts at each board meeting does not, by itself, demonstrate effective financial oversight.

Trustees need to understand what the figures mean for the charity's ability to continue operating. They also need to know when the organisation has crossed a threshold that requires intervention.

A report showing that reserves have fallen is historical information. Effective governance connects that information to a decision:

  • What caused the reduction?
  • Is it temporary or continuing?
  • How much unrestricted cash is genuinely available?
  • How long can the charity continue at its present rate of expenditure?
  • Which costs can be reduced without undermining essential services?
  • Are any assets restricted or difficult to realise?
  • What action must now be taken?
  • Who owns that action?
  • When will trustees review the position again?
Without agreed thresholds and actions, financial reporting can become a description of decline rather than a means of changing its course.

Reserves are not the same as available cash

A charity can appear to hold substantial funds while having limited money available for general operating costs.

Restricted funds must be used for their specified purposes. Property, equipment and other fixed assets cannot necessarily be converted into cash quickly. Designated funds might have been set aside for a particular purpose, although trustees can sometimes reconsider those designations.

Trustees therefore need to distinguish between:

  • total funds
  • restricted funds
  • unrestricted funds
  • designated funds
  • immediately available cash
  • assets that cannot be readily used to meet current liabilities

A headline reserves figure can create false reassurance if trustees do not understand what is actually available to support continuing operations.

Warning indicators trustees should receive

The appropriate measures will depend on the charity's size, activities and funding model. A proportionate financial-resilience report should normally include the following.

Unrestricted cash

How much cash is available to meet general operating costs? This should exclude money that the charity is legally required to use for another purpose.

Monthly operating position

Is the charity generating a surplus or deficit each month? A single monthly deficit might be manageable. A continuing deficit requires an explanation, a forecast and an agreed response.

Reserves runway

How many months can the charity continue operating if its present income and expenditure patterns continue? This calculation should use realistic assumptions and should be updated when circumstances change.

Funding concentration

How dependent is the charity on one funder, contract, donor, campaign or income stream? The board should understand the effect of losing or reducing each significant source of income.

Expected income and confidence

Not all income in a pipeline is equally likely to arrive. Trustees should be able to distinguish between secured income, formally committed income, applications awaiting a decision, anticipated renewals, early-stage opportunities and assumptions that have not yet been tested.

Liabilities and commitments

Trustees need visibility of payroll, tax, rent, supplier commitments, grant obligations, loan repayments and other amounts falling due. Long-term commitments should not be considered separately from the charity's likely future income.

Restricted assets and property

The board should understand whether restrictions, trusts, grant conditions or legal requirements affect how property and other assets can be used or disposed of. Professional advice and Charity Commission authority can take time to obtain. These questions should not first be examined when an urgent transaction has become necessary.

Agree escalation thresholds in advance

A dashboard becomes useful only when its indicators lead to action. Trustees should agree thresholds that trigger increasing levels of review. Examples might include:

  • unrestricted cash falling below a specified amount
  • reserves runway falling below an agreed number of months
  • a material funder withdrawing or delaying support
  • expenditure exceeding budget by an agreed percentage
  • payroll or tax obligations becoming difficult to meet
  • a forecast showing continuing deficits without a funded recovery plan
  • overdue actions remaining incomplete after a board deadline

The thresholds should be appropriate to the charity rather than copied from another organisation. Each threshold should identify the person who must be notified, the information that must be prepared, the meeting or decision required, whether professional advice is needed, the person responsible for each resulting action and the date on which trustees will review progress.

This turns a risk indicator into a governance process.

How Microsoft 365 can support the process

Technology should support trustee judgement, not replace it. A small charity does not necessarily need an expensive financial-management project. It can often improve visibility and accountability using Microsoft 365 applications it already has.

Excel or Power BI for financial indicators

Excel can calculate and present measures such as unrestricted cash, monthly surplus or deficit, reserves runway, budget variance, income concentration and expected income by confidence level. Power BI can provide interactive reporting where the charity has suitable licences, reliable source data and enough reporting maturity to maintain it. A simpler, accurate spreadsheet is better than a sophisticated dashboard that nobody owns or trusts.

Microsoft Lists for risks and actions

Microsoft Lists can hold a structured register containing the warning indicator, current status, agreed threshold, required action, action owner, deadline, evidence, escalation status and review date. This provides a clearer operational record than actions scattered through emails and meeting minutes.

Power Automate for reminders and escalation

Power Automate can notify action owners before deadlines and escalate overdue actions to the appropriate manager or trustee. Automation should support an agreed process. It cannot correct unclear ownership, unrealistic deadlines or decisions that nobody is prepared to enforce. Flows also need an identified owner so that they continue working when a staff member leaves or changes role.

SharePoint for controlled evidence

SharePoint can provide a controlled location for management accounts, cash-flow forecasts, budgets, funding agreements, property documents, professional advice, board papers, approved minutes, recovery plans and evidence that agreed actions were completed. Permissions should reflect the sensitivity of financial and employment information. Trustees should receive access to the information they require without exposing confidential material more widely than necessary.

Teams for trustee collaboration

A private Team can give trustees one place to access current papers, discuss issues and prepare for meetings. Important decisions should still be recorded formally. A conversation in Teams is not a substitute for approved minutes, a written resolution or another required decision record.

Preserve the evidence behind the decision

When a charity is under financial pressure, it is especially important to demonstrate how trustees reached their decisions. The record should show what information trustees received, whether forecasts and assumptions were challenged, what alternatives were considered, what professional advice was obtained, whether conflicts of interest were declared and managed, why the selected course of action was considered to be in the charity's best interests, which actions were assigned and when progress would be reviewed.

Good records do not guarantee that every decision will succeed. They demonstrate that trustees acted deliberately, used appropriate information and responded to the circumstances they faced.

Begin while options are still available

Financial resilience is not achieved by producing a dashboard after a crisis has begun. It comes from identifying the measures that matter, reviewing them consistently and agreeing what happens when a threshold is crossed.

The Charity Commission's warning is therefore not only about organisations already in severe difficulty. It is a prompt for every board to ask:

  • How early would we see a serious financial problem?
  • Which indicators would alert us?
  • What action would each indicator trigger?
  • Where is the supporting evidence?
  • Who is responsible for making sure the action happens?

Microsoft 365 can help make those answers visible and repeatable. Trustees must still exercise judgement, seek advice when necessary and make the decisions. The purpose of the technology is to help them do so before the emergency removes their choices.
