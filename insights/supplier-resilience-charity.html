When a critical supplier goes offline: a charity's guide to operational resilience

July 2026
7 min read
Adjona Technology

Recent disruption affecting thousands of UK charities following the temporary suspension of CAF Bank's online banking service is a timely reminder that operational resilience extends beyond your own organisation. Even when your internal systems are secure, the failure of a critical supplier can quickly affect payments, payroll, communications and service delivery.

For trustees and charity leaders, the lesson is not about one particular bank. It is about understanding how dependent modern organisations have become on third-party services — and asking a simple question:

Could our charity continue operating if one of our most important suppliers became unavailable tomorrow?

Every charity depends on external suppliers

Most charities rely on a network of organisations to deliver day-to-day operations. These commonly include banking providers, Microsoft 365, email services, payroll providers, accounting systems, CRM platforms, donation platforms, website hosting and internet connectivity.

These services often work so reliably that they become invisible. However, when one becomes unavailable, the impact can be immediate.

The issue is not whether disruption will happen. Every organisation experiences incidents, whether caused by cyber attacks, technical failures, supplier outages or human error. The real question is whether your charity has prepared for them.

Business continuity is a governance responsibility

Business continuity is often viewed as an IT issue. In reality, trustees have a responsibility to ensure that essential services can continue during unexpected disruption. Technology supports resilience, but governance determines how effectively an organisation responds.

Ask yourself:

  • How would urgent supplier invoices be paid?
  • Could payroll still be processed?
  • How would trustees communicate if email became unavailable?
  • Are emergency contact details accessible without relying on cloud systems?
  • Who has delegated authority to make decisions during an incident?
  • Which services must be restored first?
These are governance decisions, not technical ones.

Reduce risk before disruption occurs

No organisation can eliminate every risk. What separates resilient organisations from vulnerable ones is preparation.

A practical resilience review should include:

  • Identifying critical suppliers and understanding your dependence on them
  • Recording alternative methods of contacting key suppliers
  • Documenting manual procedures for essential activities such as payments and payroll
  • Reviewing delegated authority for urgent operational decisions
  • Ensuring important governance documents remain accessible during an outage
  • Testing business continuity arrangements regularly rather than assuming they will work

Many of these measures cost little to implement but can significantly reduce operational disruption.

Technology is only part of the solution

Platforms such as Microsoft 365 provide powerful tools that support resilience — Teams, SharePoint, OneDrive and Power Platform. However, technology cannot compensate for unclear responsibilities, undocumented processes or poor governance.

If critical information cannot be found, approval processes are not understood, or key decisions rely on one individual, recovery becomes far more difficult regardless of the technology available.

Operational resilience starts with governance, supported by technology.

Questions every trustee should be able to answer

Before the next incident occurs, consider these questions:

Have we identified our critical suppliers?
Do we understand which services would stop if one became unavailable?
Do we have documented manual workarounds for essential activities such as payroll and payments?
Are emergency contact details available if our primary systems are unavailable?
Have delegated authorities been documented so urgent decisions can still be made?
Could trustees and key staff communicate if email or Microsoft 365 were unavailable?
Is our business continuity plan up to date and has it been tested within the last 12 months?
Do we know who we would contact first during a major supplier outage?

If several of these questions are difficult to answer, your charity may be more vulnerable than you realise.

Final thoughts

Operational resilience is not about predicting every incident. It is about ensuring your organisation can continue delivering its mission when the unexpected happens.

The recent disruption affecting CAF Bank serves as a useful reminder that resilience extends beyond your own systems. Your charity's ability to continue operating depends not only on the technology you use, but also on the governance, planning and decision-making that support it.

The organisations that recover most effectively are rarely those with the most technology. They are the ones that have prepared.
